## Quick Reference

* 2–8 players; S = 3n (+1 if even); Pillars = floor(1.5n); map structure is public.
* Each round, secretly choose simultaneously: on a Pillar, Hide/Observe/Attack/Slip; on a Plain, Observe/Attack/Slip.
* Hide: invisible, cannot be hit; 1 player who Slips in can kill you directly; bombardment ignores hiding.
* Observe: shine on L segments; if a segment contains someone other than you and that someone is not "Pillar + Hide," a red dot lights up; the observed player does not know.
* Attack: shoot one segment for 1 damage on a hit; reveals a half-plane to every other player (even on a Plain, no exact segment is shown—only side and count); Attack can be interrupted by Slip; in a mutual attack (both on Pillars), the longer arc takes no damage, and equal arcs kill both.
* Slip: move 1 segment (2 from a Plain); kill a Hider, an Attacker, or an Observer who did not see you coming; transit segments do not count.
* Multiple players in the same segment: the shortest movement distance stays; tied shortest all die.
* Bombardment: first warning on round 2, then once every 3 rounds from round 4; it indiscriminately bombs a whole segment, and players on that segment die; afterward the segment becomes a Ruins area (cannot be entered, stayed in, or passed through; only an adjacent Plain side can pass straight through).
* Resolution: Hide → Observe → Slip → Attack → death; the last player wins.

## 1. Game Concept

*Dark Forest* is a 2–8 player simultaneous-action, hidden-information, ring-map hunting game. Each round, all players secretly choose an action and resolve it together; the last player alive wins.

In one sentence: in a ring-shaped dark forest, everyone is shining a flashlight, firing shots, and moving in the dark. The flashlight illuminates others—and it also reveals you.

## 2. Terminology

* **n**: the number of players, 2 ≤ n ≤ 8.
* **Segment**: the smallest unit of the map; all segments join end to end to form a ring.
* **S**: the total number of segments.
* **Pillar**: special terrain on which a player can **Hide**.
* **Plain**: ordinary terrain where a player cannot **Hide**, but has better vision and movement.
* **Ruins area**: a segment after bombardment. It cannot be occupied; normal movement cannot enter or pass through it, nor stop on it; only from an adjacent **Plain** side can a player pass straight through.
* **Exposure arc**: a contiguous arc of segments covered by one **Observe** or **Attack**.
* **Half-plane**: relative to a given player, whether the attacker is on that player's clockwise side or counterclockwise side.
* **Movement distance**: the number of segments moved this round; staying in place counts as 0. Used for the crowding resolution in 5.5.
* **Round-start position**: the position used for this round's **Observe** determination; movement takes effect only after **Observe**.

## 3. Map

### 3.1 Segment Count

S = 3n; if 3n is even, then S = 3n + 1.

So S is always odd. Any two segments have a unique shorter arc, so there is no "exactly halfway" draw.

(The original text says, "if 3n is not a cardinal number, change it to 3n + 1"; "cardinal number" is understood here as "odd number.")

### 3.2 Number of Pillars

P = floor(1.5n), distributed randomly; the remaining S − P segments are Plains.

### 3.3 Player Count Reference

* 2 players: S = 7, P = 3
* 3 players: S = 9, P = 4
* 4 players: S = 13, P = 6
* 5 players: S = 15, P = 7
* 6 players: S = 19, P = 9
* 7 players: S = 21, P = 10
* 8 players: S = 25, P = 12

### 3.4 Initial Positions and Public Information

All players are placed randomly on distinct segments; the map structure (whether each segment is a Pillar or a Plain) is public from the start. Ruins areas that appear later are also public.

## 4. Round Flow

### 4.1 Selection

All living players simultaneously and secretly choose this round's action. The available actions are determined by the terrain at their **round-start position**:

* On a Pillar: Hide, Observe, Attack, or Slip—choose one.
* On a Plain: Observe, Attack, Slip, or Hide (empty)—choose one.
* A Ruins area cannot be an action starting point; a player cannot be in a Ruins area.

### 4.2 Resolution Order

Each round proceeds in the following order:

0. **Round start**: handle this round's bombardment and warning per 10.1 (round 2 warns of round 4; on a bombardment round, resolve the bombardment first, then issue the next warning). Players then choose actions.
1. **Hide**: confirm all hiding states.
2. **Observe**: calculate observation results using round-start positions and send them privately to the observer.
3. **Slip**: all movement takes effect simultaneously, positions are updated, kills caused by movement are resolved, and multiple players in the same segment are handled per 5.5; special movement through a Ruins area is handled per 10.4.
4. **Attack**: resolve shooting using post-movement positions. If an **Attack** player is killed by **Slip** this round, that player's **Attack** is canceled and has no effect.
5. **End of round**: resolve all deaths together.

Four easily confused points:

* **Observe** sees positions **before movement**.
* **Slip** kills happen before **Attack**; a player killed by **Slip** does not get to fire.
* **Attack** damage takes effect only at the end of the round, so players who shoot each other can die together.
* When the **Attack** phase begins, each segment holds at most 1 player (see 5.5); no one is on a Ruins area.

## 5. Pillar Actions

### 5.1 Hide (effective on a Pillar; an empty action on a Plain)

* You perform no other action.
* **On a Pillar**: you are not found by **Observe** and cannot be hit by **Attack**.
* **On a Plain**: you may choose **Hide**, but it has no effect—you are not invisible, you do not block observation, and you do not block shots; it is merely a stationary empty action. There is no cover on a Plain.
* If only 1 player **Slips** into your segment this round, you are killed directly (whether you are on a Pillar or a Plain).
* If 2 or more players **Slip** into your segment this round, handle it per 5.5: you stay in place with movement distance 0, which is the shortest distance, so you survive and all who Slip in die.
* Bombardment ignores hiding: if your segment is bombed, you die (see 10.3).
* You still receive public information (for example, half-plane exposures caused by another player's **Attack**).

### 5.2 Observe

* Starting from your own segment, choose clockwise or counterclockwise and specify the number of observed segments L: at least 1 segment. **On a Plain, at most S segments (the whole ring); on a Pillar, at most floor(S/2) segments (a half ring)**. A full ring is not allowed from a Pillar.
* These L segments (including your own) are this round's **exposure arc**.
* For each segment in range: if that segment contains a player other than you, and that player is not both "on a Pillar and Hiding," a red dot lights up on that segment.
* It shows only "occupied/unoccupied," not who is there.
* The result is sent only to the observer; the observed player receives no notice.
* Illuminating the entire ring from a Plain means any attacker is within your arc; under the damage test in 5.3, you are more likely to be hit by **Attack**. A Pillar is limited to a half ring at most and cannot cover the whole ring.

### 5.3 Attack

* Designate a segment as the target and choose clockwise or counterclockwise direction.
* The number of segments from the attacker's segment to the target segment along the chosen direction (excluding the starting segment, including the target segment) is this attack's arc length; in a mutual attack, this length is compared.
* **Damage test**:

  * Target is **Hiding** → takes no damage.
  * Target is **Observing** or **Attacking**, and its **exposure arc** does not contain the attacker's segment (its profile is not exposed to the attacker) → takes no damage.
  * Otherwise → takes 1 damage.
  * The author's confirmation: the less of your profile you expose, the fewer firing lines you naturally take.

* **Exposure**: every other player separately learns the attacker's **half-plane** relative to themselves: if the attacker is reached first by going clockwise, it is recorded as the "left half"; if first by going counterclockwise, the "right half." This exposure occurs whether or not the shot hits.
* **Mutual attack**: when two players on Pillars **Attack** each other, the one with the longer arc takes no damage from the shorter one; the shorter one resolves normally, and attacks by other players also resolve normally. If the lengths are equal, both take damage (with 1 HP, both die). The mutual-attack rule applies only when both players are on Pillars; a Pillar-versus-Plain or Plain-versus-Plain exchange uses the ordinary damage test.
* **Interrupted**: if the initiator of an **Attack** is killed by **Slip** this round, the **Attack** is canceled, has no effect, and produces no half-plane exposure.

### 5.4 Slip

* Move 1 segment left or right; the ends wrap around.
* If, after moving, there is someone in the segment and any of the following applies, kill that player:

  1. That player is **Hiding** or **Attacking**;
  2. That player is **Observing**, and its **exposure arc** does not contain the segment the mover was in before moving (it did not see you coming).
* If two or more players Slip into the same segment after moving, do not resolve them one by one under this section; use 5.5 instead.
* A player killed by **Slip** has this round's **Attack** canceled.
* Normal movement cannot enter a Ruins area; see 10.4 for special crossing of a Ruins area.

### 5.5 Multiple Players in the Same Segment and Crowding Resolution

Because **Slip** resolves before **Attack**, multiple players in the same segment must be fully handled during the **Slip** phase; when the **Attack** phase begins, each segment holds at most 1 player.

1. **1 player who Slips in + 1 original occupant**: resolve per 5.4. If the original occupant does not die (is **Observing** and saw the approach), the player who Slips in is killed—they ran into a prepared player and cannot remain in the same segment.
2. **2 or more players who Slip in**: do not resolve 5.4 one by one; instead, compare the **movement distance** of all players in that segment (staying in place counts as 0).

   * The player with the shortest movement distance stays; all other players die.
   * If several players tie for the shortest distance, all of them die (the segment may end up empty), and farther players also die.

The original three-player rule is superseded by this rule: 1 player who stays in place (distance 0) + 2 players who Slip in (distance at least 1) → the stationary player has the shortest distance, and all who Slip in die.

## 6. Plain Actions

A player on a Plain may **Hide**, but it is only an empty action: it does not make you invisible, does not block observation, and does not block shots (see 5.1).

### 6.1 Observe

* Sees all segments; the **exposure arc** is effectively the whole ring.
* The test is the same as 5.2: players hiding on a Pillar remain invisible; a player on a Plain appears in observation red dots regardless of which action they choose (Observe/Attack/Slip).

### 6.2 Attack

* Designate a segment to shoot at; the attack arc is still calculated along the chosen direction (used for preview and mutual-attack comparison).
* A gunshot does not publicly reveal the exact segment or exact position. Every other living player receives only two pieces of information: whether the gunshot came from their left half or right half, and how many shots were fired on each side this round.
* Whether or not the shot hits, the gunshot produces the half-plane information above; multiple shots in the same round are accumulated separately for left and right.
* There is no cover on a Plain: the damage test is the same as 5.3. When a player on a Plain is attacked, the exposure arc of Observe/Attack is the whole ring anyway, and Slip provides no cover either; this is separate from a gunshot giving only the left/right side.
* A Plain does not participate in the "both players on Pillars" mutual-attack protection; a Pillar-versus-Plain exchange uses the ordinary damage test.

### 6.3 Slip

* Base movement is 1 segment.
* Extra effect: if the adjacent segment in the direction of movement is a **Plain**, you may move 1 more segment, for a total of 2.
* Only the final landing segment is checked; transit segments trigger no effects (no kills, no collisions, no observation).
* The kill test is the same as 5.4; multiple players in the same segment use 5.5.
* **A Ruins area cannot be used as the intermediate segment of an ordinary 2-step Slip**: a player on a Plain can cross a Ruins area only through the special passage in 10.4.

## 7. Information and Publicity

* Map structure (which segments are Pillars and which are Plains): public.
* Bombardment warnings and Ruins status (which segment will be bombed when, and which segments are already Ruins areas): public.
* Player positions: not public; initial positions are all distinct.
* **Observe** results: known only to the observer; the observed player does not know they were observed.
* **Attack** exposure: every other player gets a half-plane clue relative to themselves; an **Attack** from a Plain also gives only the half-plane, not the exact segment.
* Deaths: public.
* The killer's identity: not public; deaths caused by bombardment are recorded as "bombardment," not as a player.
* There is no chat/communication channel in an official match; players and AI may act only on their own private information.

## 8. Damage, Death, and Victory

* Players have only 1 HP; a hit is fatal.
* Multiple players can die in the same round; when mutual-attack arcs are equal, both take damage, meaning both die.
* Deaths caused by bombardment also count; a player still in a target segment when it is bombed dies.
* A Ruins area cannot be occupied; players cannot stay in a Ruins area.
* The dead are removed at the end of the round.
* When only 1 player remains, that player wins; the winner takes all.
* If all remaining players die in the same round (including a bombardment wipe), the result is a draw.

## 9. Resolution Details

1. An **Attack** shoots a "segment," not a "person." Whoever is in that segment when it resolves is hit; an empty segment means a miss. 5.5 guarantees at most 1 player per segment, so one shot cannot hit multiple players.
2. **Observe** range: on a Plain, 1 to S; on a Pillar, 1 to floor(S/2). Only a Plain can cover the whole ring.
3. Movement kills are based on the **post-movement** landing segment. If the original occupant also Slips away this round, the landing segment is empty and no kill occurs.
4. Multiple players in the same segment are always resolved per 5.5; co-occupancy of a segment is no longer allowed.
5. When a player on a Plain Slips 2 segments, only the final landing segment is checked; transit segments trigger no effects.
6. An **Attack** player killed by **Slip** produces no half-plane exposure, because the attack never happened.
7. **Observe** information comes before death resolution; an observer still gets the observation result even if they die this round.
8. A player cannot attack their own segment.
9. There are no players in a Ruins area, so it does not participate in 5.5 crowding; a player crossing a Ruins area participates in 5.4/5.5 at the **landing segment**.
10. See 10.4 for Ruins passage rules; normal movement cannot enter or pass through.

## 10. Bombardment and Ruins Areas

### 10.1 Schedule

* The starting player count is **n**; the number of living players is **alive**.
* **The opening schedule is unchanged**: no warning on round 1; at the start of round 2, warn of round 4; at the start of round 4, resolve the first bombardment.
* After each bombardment resolves, the next warning is determined by the current `alive` count:

  * `alive > ceil(n/2)`: the interval between warning and bombardment is **3 rounds**, with 1 target each time (the original rule);
  * `alive ≤ ceil(n/2)` and `alive > ceil(n/4)`: the interval changes to **2 rounds**, with 1 target each time;
  * `alive ≤ ceil(n/4)`: the interval becomes **3 rounds** again, but each warning names **2 different targets**, which explode simultaneously when due.
* Thresholds are evaluated **when the next warning is issued**, using the alive count at that moment; an already-issued warning is not changed retroactively.
* Example: in an 8-player game, when 4 players are alive, the next warning uses a 2-round interval; when 2 players are alive, the next warning comes 3 rounds later and names two segments at once.
* If the game ends before a bombardment round, that bombardment does not occur.

### 10.2 Target Selection

* A bombardment target is a **whole segment**, not the boundary between two segments.
* Targets are chosen when the warning is issued: randomly select 1 or 2 segments from all **not yet bombed** segments, **regardless of Pillar/Plain**.
* In dual-zone mode, select 2 **different** segments; if fewer than 2 segments are available, select as many as possible.
* A segment containing players may be selected; bombardment is **indiscriminate**.
* The same segment is never bombed twice.
* If there are no segments left that have not been bombed, bombardment stops.

### 10.3 Bombardment Resolution

* Resolve at the **start** of the bombardment round, before players choose actions.
* If this warning has 2 targets, both targets resolve **simultaneously**.
* Players still in any target segment die; bombardment is **indiscriminate**: it ignores **Hide**/**Observe**/**Attack**/**Slip**, does not distinguish Pillar/Plain, and cannot be avoided by any action.
* Each target segment separately becomes a **Ruins area** and is handled per 10.4 from this round onward.
* Deaths are public; the kill credit is recorded as "bombardment," not as a player.
* If bombardment causes the last player to die, handle it under Section 8.

### 10.4 Passage Through Ruins Areas

* A Ruins area cannot be occupied, and normal movement cannot enter or pass through it; players cannot stay in a Ruins area.
* **Special passage**: if a Ruins area is adjacent to a **Plain**, a player standing on that **Plain** may, when **Slipping**, choose to **pass straight through** the Ruins area and land on the segment on the other side.

  * The player does not stay in the Ruins area; crossing **does not count as entering**.
  * This is a 2-segment move: starting from the Plain segment → through the Ruins area → landing on the other side.
  * A player can cross only when starting from the **Plain** side; crossing from the **Pillar** side is not allowed.
  * The other side may be a **Pillar** or a **Plain**; it **cannot be another Ruins area** (you cannot land in Ruins).
  * Crossing triggers no mid-move effects: no kills, no collisions, no observation.
  * If the landing segment is occupied, resolve it as one 2-segment **Slip** (5.4 + 5.5).
* If both sides of the Ruins area are **Pillars**: there is no special passage; it is a hard wall.
* If one side is a **Plain** and the other is a **Pillar**: the passage is one-way, only Plain → Pillar; the Pillar side cannot cross in reverse.
* If both sides are **Plains**: crossing is allowed in both directions.
* If one side is a **Plain** and the other is another Ruins area: crossing is impossible (you cannot land in Ruins).
* A Ruins area does not block **Observe** and **Attack**: you can observe/shoot the Ruins area and the other side; it is only a hole for movement.
* There are no players in a Ruins area, and no one can **Hide** there; anyone still in the target segment when it is bombed dies.

### 10.5 Relationship to Other Rules

* No one can **Hide** in a Ruins area; if someone is hiding there when it is bombed, they still die.
* Crowding resolution follows 5.5: there are no players in a Ruins area, so it does not participate; a crosser participates in 5.4/5.5 at the **landing segment**.
* The movement distance of a crossing is 2; if the landing segment is already occupied, resolve per item 1 of 5.5: if the landing player is **Observing** and saw the crosser's **departure segment**, the crosser dies; if the landing player is **Attacking**, the landing player dies; if the landing player is **Hiding**, the landing player dies.
* A Ruins area does not count as being "adjacent to a **Plain**" for this purpose: the special passage recognizes only actual **Plains**; a Ruins area is not itself a **Plain**.
* A Ruins area never becomes a bombardment target again (it has already been bombed).

### 10.6 Victory

* Death by bombardment does not change the victory condition: the last player wins; if bombardment causes all remaining players to die simultaneously, the result is a draw.

### 10.7 Confirmed Ruins Parameters (v1.0.0)

1. **Pillar–Pillar Ruins**: remains a hard wall and cannot be crossed; the bombardment target is not rerolled just because both sides are Pillars. The map may contain impassable dead zones.
2. **Consecutive Ruins**: keep the current interpretation; consecutive Ruins are treated as a thick wall and cannot be crossed in one move; a player may only cross a **single** Ruins from an adjacent **Plain** side to the other side.
3. **Bombardment timing**: remains at the start of the round, before players act.
4. **First pacing**: keep the special 2-round opening preparation—warning on round 2, first bombardment on round 4.
5. **2–3 player games**: the first bombardment remains on round 4 and is not moved earlier to round 3.
6. **Threshold changes**: remain non-retroactive; an already-issued warning does not change its targets. Thresholds are evaluated when the next warning is issued, using the alive count at that moment.

## 11. Version Notes and Confirmed Interpretations

### 11.1 Confirmed Rulings

1. **Hide and Slip**: a Hider is killed by Slip; the first Slip condition applies to "Hiding."
2. **Attack damage**: the literal reading—target is **Observing**/**Attacking** and its exposure arc does not contain the attacker → takes no damage. The author's exact words: "The less of your profile you expose, the fewer firing lines you naturally take."
3. **HP**: 1 point; a hit is fatal.
4. **Attack and Slip**: Attack does not grant immunity to Slip; an Attacker killed by Slip has its **Attack** canceled (when two or more players Slip in, a stationary **Attacker** instead survives under 5.5).
5. **Equal-length mutual attack**: both sides take damage; with 1 HP, both die.
6. **Multiple players in the same segment**: resolve per 5.5 by movement distance; the shortest stays, and tied shortest all die. The original three-player rule is superseded by this rule.
7. **Attack from a Plain**: a gunshot gives only the left/right side and the count, not the exact segment; the attack arc is still calculated by direction; a Plain does not participate in Pillar–Pillar mutual-attack protection.
8. **Transit segments**: when a player on a Plain Slips 2 segments, transit segments trigger no effects.
9. **Information**: map structure is public; initial positions are distinct; the killer is not public; an observed player does not know they were observed.
10. **Full-ring Observe**: allowed only **on a Plain**; on a Pillar, at most `floor(S/2)` segments, never the whole ring. A full ring from a Plain means any attacker is within your arc, making you easier to hit under item 2.
11. **Bombardment**: the target is a whole segment; it is indiscriminate, any unbombed segment may be selected, and players on the segment die regardless of state; the first warning is on round 2.
12. **Ruins area**: cannot be entered, occupied, or crossed by normal movement; only from an adjacent **Plain** side can a player pass straight through to the other side, and crossing does not count as entering; a Ruins area does not block Observe/Attack.

### 11.2 Open Items

None. v1.0.0 has closed all provisional items; in 2–3 player games, the first bombardment remains on round 4.

### 11.3 Examples

**Half-plane determination**

S = 13, the attacker is on segment 1, and you are on segment 5.

* Clockwise: 5 → 6 → … → 13 → 1, 9 segments in total.
* Counterclockwise: 5 → 4 → 3 → 2 → 1, 4 segments in total.

Counterclockwise reaches the attacker first, so the information you receive is "the attacker is in the right half."

**One-round resolution**

n = 3, S = 9. A is on segment 1 (Pillar), B is on segment 2 (Pillar), and C is on segment 5 (Plain).

* A chooses **Slip** to the right; B chooses **Hide**; C chooses **Attack**, targeting segment 2.
* Resolution: B is hiding; A Slips into segment 2, where B is Hiding, so B is killed; C's **Attack** is aimed at segment 2, where A now is; A is **Slipping**, neither Hiding nor Observing/Attacking, so A is hit and dies; C is **Attacking** from a Plain, so position 5 is exposed to everyone.
* End of round: A and B are dead, and C wins.

This example also shows that an attack targets a "segment," and a player who Slips in can take the bullet intended for the original occupant.

**Bombardment and Ruins areas**

At the start of round 2, a public warning announces that segment 3 will be bombed on round 4. At the start of round 4, segment 3 is bombed: anyone still in that segment dies; segment 3 becomes a Ruins area, which cannot be entered, occupied, or crossed by normal movement.

If segment 3's neighbors are segment 2 (Plain) and segment 4 (Pillar):

* A player on segment 2 can, when **Slipping**, pass straight through segment 3 and land on segment 4; this is a 2-segment move, the crossing does not count as entering, and the player does not stay on segment 3.
* A player on segment 4 cannot cross segment 3 in reverse, because they are on the **Pillar** side.
* If both segments 2 and 4 are **Plains**, crossing is allowed in both directions.

On round 4, a warning is also issued that segment 7 will be bombed on round 7.

## 12. Online Rooms (v1.0.0)

* A room holds at most **8 players (humans + AI)**; the total is not preselected, humans may join over time, and the host may add/remove AI seats.
* Each AI seat can be assigned a model individually or set to "random"; the random pool excludes the Night Watchman baseline, which only Hides/Observes the full ring, but the Night Watchman can still be selected manually.
* The host sets the per-round action wait limit, ranging from **20 seconds to 3 minutes**, default 60 seconds; it is locked after the match starts.
* Only human action time is counted; AI and humans begin thinking at the same time, and after all humans submit, resolution waits for the AI to finish.
* A timeout is treated as **Hide**; Hide on a Plain is only an empty action and does not make you invisible or block shots.
* After each round's resolution, a "Round X Review" opens automatically (this round's private intel + public resolution); the result phase auto-continues after 10 seconds, and living humans can also click "Continue" early.
* **Eliminated players** automatically enter god-view spectating: they can see all player positions and public events but cannot act; the complete replay after the match ends is saved to local match history.
* **When only AI remains**, the server simulates directly to the end and no longer waits for eliminated humans to continue; after the match record is fully saved, all online players return directly to the room. The room retains human/AI seats, AI models, timing settings, and the room code; **the next match does not start automatically**, and the host must click "Start Match" manually.
* **Silent competition mode**: there is no chat, voice, quick chat, or marker channel during a match; the first version has no disconnect reconnection/automated control.
* **Disconnect handling**: during an ongoing match (started and the player is alive), if the client disconnects:

  * that player's action for this round is treated as **Hide**;
  * the player dies immediately, with the cause of death recorded as "disconnect";
  * a public broadcast announces "X died due to disconnect";
  * the corpse is recorded as a normal death;
  * if the player is the host, the room still closes (the room closes when the host leaves);
  * there is no disconnect reconnection/seat recovery.

## 13. Corpse Markers (v0.1.9)

* After a player dies, a corpse remains where they died; a corpse does not block movement, observation, or attacks, and does not change the terrain.
* **Living players**:

  * see only corpse markers in **their current segment**;
  * know the public list of deaths (who died), but not the segments where other corpses lie;
  * while standing on a corpse segment, the marker shows the current corpse count on that segment and **the round in which they discovered it**;
  * do not see the true death round;
  * after the first discovery, this record is retained; on returning to the same segment, if the corpse count has increased, update the count and the most recent discovery round;
  * after leaving that segment, this corpse information is not shown in other segments.
* **Eliminated spectators/replays**:

  * god view shows all corpses, counts, true death rounds, and the names of the dead;
  * replays no longer draw a separate token for each dead player; instead, corpses are aggregated by segment into corpse markers to avoid stacking in the same cell.
* Deaths caused by bombardment also leave corpses; after the segment becomes Ruins, the corpses remain.
* Corpse counts and discovery rounds are the local player's memory, not public map-wide information; the only public information is "who died."

## 14. Firing Feedback (v0.2.0)

* The attacker knows only which segment they fired at, in which direction, and their attack arc length.
* The system does not feed back to the attacker:

  * whether the shot was a miss, was dodged, hit, or was suppressed by a longer arc;
  * whether the target was in the target segment;
  * whether the target died from this shot.
* When the target is hit/killed, the target receives the damage/death result; the public log shows only "who died," not who fired or from where.
* Players and AI can infer firing effects only from public deaths, corpse discoveries, later observations, and actions.
* The complete attack result (attacker, target segment, arc length, hit/miss/dodged/suppressed) is stored only in the god-view replay and is not sent to any player or AI during the match.

## 15. Startup/Connection Recovery (v1.0.0)

* If `start.bat` fails to start or the server exits abnormally, it retries automatically, up to 3 times.
* Errors are written to `logs\server-errors.log`.
* If the browser disconnects from the server, it attempts to reconnect; repeated failures show a "Connection lost" notice and a "Refresh to reconnect" button instead of leaving only a black screen.
* A page script error shows a "Page error" notice and a refresh button.
* Reconnection restores only the connection to the main menu/lobby, not an ongoing room (this game still does not support in-match reconnection).
* After all browser tabs are closed, the server waits for a 5-second grace period; a refresh/reconnect during this time cancels shutdown; after the timeout the server exits with code 0, and start.bat's hidden watchdog ends with it.
* Closing only one tab does not exit; Ollama/local GPU inference services are independent services and do not shut down with the game server.
