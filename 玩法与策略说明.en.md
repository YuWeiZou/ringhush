# Dark Forest: Gameplay and Basic Strategy

> English | \[中文](./玩法与策略说明.md)

This document describes only the rules, information structure, decision space, and common strategies; it does not evaluate the quality or entertainment value of the game.  
The rule baseline is the *Rulebook* v0.1.7.

## 1\. Game Overview

* **Players**: 2–8, with humans and AI able to occupy seats together; the total includes AI.
* **Map**: a ring-shaped map whose ends join, made up of a number of "segments."
* **Objective**: survive to the end. The last remaining survivor wins; if everyone dies in the same round, the result is a draw.
* **How actions work**: each round, all living players simultaneously and secretly choose one action, and the server resolves them together.
* **Communication**: there is no chat, voice, quick chat, or marker channel during a match; this is a silent competition mode.
* **Information**: players know only their own position, their own observation results, and the clues they receive; other players' positions are not public.

In one sentence:

On a ring-shaped map, all players simultaneously hide, observe, move, and shoot, using limited and individually different information to judge others' positions and actions and survive to the end.

## 2\. Map and Terrain

### 2.1 Segment Count

* The number of players is `n`.
* The total number of segments `S` is `3n`; if `3n` is even, use `3n + 1`.
* Therefore `S` is always odd, ensuring that the "half-plane" determination never results in a tie.

Examples:

* Players n: 2; segments S: 7; Pillars P: 3
* Players n: 4; segments S: 13; Pillars P: 6
* Players n: 8; segments S: 25; Pillars P: 12

### 2.2 Terrain

* **Pillar**: allows **Hide**; while hiding, a player is not found by **Observe** and cannot be hit by **Attack**.
* **Plain**: cannot gain a hiding effect; but movement and observation are more flexible, and an **Attack** from a Plain exposes the whole ring.
* **Ruins area**: a segment after bombardment. It cannot be entered, occupied, or crossed by normal movement; only a player standing on an adjacent **Plain** can pass straight through while **Slipping**. Ruins do not block **Observe** or **Attack**.

Map structure (segment count, Pillar count, and whether initial positions are duplicated) is public information; players' exact positions are not public.

## 3\. The Four Actions

Each round, choose one of the following actions. On a Pillar, you may choose Hide/Observe/Attack/Slip; on a Plain, you may choose Observe/Attack/Slip, and you may also choose "Hide," but Hide on a Plain produces no hiding effect.

### 3.1 Hide

* **On a Pillar**:

  * not found by **Observe**;
  * not hit by **Attack**;
  * can be killed by **Slip**;
  * can be killed by bombardment.
* **On a Plain**:

  * not invisible, does not block observation, and does not block shots;
  * you remain a stationary, undefended target, and are killed if a **Slip** enters your segment;
  * its main use is to "stand by in place," usually as a timeout fallback.

### 3.2 Observe

* Starting from your own segment, choose clockwise or counterclockwise and specify a length `L`.
* `L` is at least 1 segment and at most a full ring of `S` segments; the observation range includes your own segment.
* For each segment in range, if that segment contains a player other than you, and the other player is not both "on a Pillar + Hiding," a red dot lights up on that segment.
* It shows only "occupied/unoccupied," not who is there.
* The result is sent only to the observer; the observed player receives no notice.
* Observation from a Plain covers the whole ring; observation from a Pillar is calculated by direction and length.

### 3.3 Attack

* Designate a target segment and choose clockwise or counterclockwise direction.
* The number of segments from the attacker's segment to the target segment along the chosen direction (excluding the starting segment, including the target segment) is the attack arc length.
* Target is **Hiding** (and this is effective on a Pillar) → takes no damage.
* Target is **Observing** or **Attacking**, and its exposure arc does not contain the attacker's segment → takes no damage.
* Otherwise → the target takes 1 damage.
* After the attack, every other player separately receives one **self-referenced** half-plane clue:

  * if the attacker lies on the side reached first by going clockwise from that player, it is recorded as the "left half";
  * if on the side reached first counterclockwise, it is recorded as the "right half";
  * the exact segment is not disclosed.
* Mutual attack: when two players on Pillars attack each other, the one with the longer arc takes no damage from the shorter one; if the lengths are equal, both take damage. This rule applies only when both players are on Pillars; Pillar-versus-Plain and Plain-versus-Plain exchanges use the ordinary damage test.
* If the attacker is killed by **Slip** this round, the attack is canceled, has no effect, and produces no half-plane exposure.

### 3.4 Slip

* Move 1 segment left or right; on a Plain, you may choose to move 2 segments.
* If the landing segment is occupied and any of the following conditions is met, kill that player:

  1. the player is **Hiding** or **Attacking**;
  2. the player is **Observing**, and its observation arc does not contain the segment the mover was in before moving (it did not see the approach).
* If 2 or more players Slip into the same segment after moving, do not resolve one by one; use the "multiple players in the same segment" rule instead.
* A 2-segment move on a Plain checks only the final landing segment; transit segments trigger no effects.
* An **Attack** player killed by **Slip** has this round's attack canceled.
* Normal movement cannot enter a Ruins area; the Plain side can use the special crossing through Ruins.

### 3.5 Multiple Players in the Same Segment

* 1 player who Slips in + 1 original occupant: resolve per 3.4.
* 2 or more players who Slip in: do not resolve one by one; instead, directly compare the **movement distance** of all players in that segment:

  * staying in place counts as 0;
  * the player with the shortest movement distance stays;
  * all the others die;
  * if several players tie for the shortest distance, all of them die, and the segment may end up empty.
* Because of this rule, with 1 stationary player (distance 0) + 2 players who Slip in (distance at least 1), the stationary player survives and all who Slip in die.
* Likewise, when 2 or more players Slip into a stationary player who is **Attacking**, the Attacker survives and all who Slip in die.

## 4\. Resolution Order

Each round resolves in a fixed order:

1. **Round start**: handle bombardment and warnings;
2. **Hide**: confirm all hiding states;
3. **Observe**: determine observation results using round-start positions;
4. **Slip**: all movement takes effect simultaneously, resolve movement kills, and handle multiple players in the same segment;
5. **Attack**: resolve shooting using post-movement positions; an Attacker killed by Slip this round has its attack canceled;
6. **Death**: resolve damage and deaths;
7. check victory.

Key points:

* Observation uses the **round-start position**;
* movement takes effect after observation;
* Attack uses the **post-movement position**;
* Slip kills happen before Attack.

## 5\. Bombardment and Ruins

* First warning on round 2: bombardment on round 4; the first pacing is unchanged.
* Afterward, based on the number of living players when the next warning is issued: `alive > ceil(n/2)` gives a 3-round interval with a single target; `≤ ceil(n/2)` and `> ceil(n/4)` gives a 2-round interval with a single target; `≤ ceil(n/4)` gives a 3-round interval with two different targets each time, exploding simultaneously.
* Targets are chosen randomly from all segments that are not currently Ruins, regardless of Pillar/Plain and regardless of whether a segment is occupied.
* At the start of a bombardment round, all players on the target segments die, ignoring Hide/Observe/Attack/Slip and ignoring Pillar/Plain.
* Each such segment then becomes a Ruins area:

  * it cannot be entered, occupied, or crossed by normal movement;
  * it does not block **Observe** or **Attack**;
  * only a player standing on an adjacent **Plain** can pass straight through while **Slipping**, landing on the other side;
  * crossing counts as a 2-segment move, and the landing segment participates in 3.4/3.5 resolution.

## 6\. Information Structure

### 6.1 Public Information

* Map segment count, Pillar count, and Ruins distribution;
* bombardment warnings and bombardment results;
* death events and causes of death (public log);
* the public death list (who died); corpse locations elsewhere are not public; living players see only the corpse count in their current segment and know only the round in which they discovered it;
* the counts of "left/right half-plane" clues produced by each attack;
* the current round number, timer, and number of living players;
* player nicknames, AI models, and the final winner.

### 6.2 Private Information

* your own position and terrain;
* your own observation results (which segments have red dots);
* the half-plane clues you received;
* the target segment and arc length of your own shot; **there is no feedback on whether the shot was a miss, blocked, a hit, or suppressed in a mutual attack**; you learn you were hit only when you take damage/die.

### 6.3 The Nature of Half-Plane Clues

An attack does not publicly reveal the attacker's exact segment; instead, it gives every other player a "left/right" constraint **relative to that player themselves**.

* Different players may receive different left/right indications;
* multiple half-plane clues can intersect to narrow the range of segments where the attacker might be;
* a clue gives only a "side," not a distance;
* the attacker knows they have been exposed, but not exactly what constraints others received.

### 6.4 Communication

* There is no chat, voice, quick chat, or marker channel during a match.
* Players cannot exchange half-plane clues, positions, intentions, or alliances.
* AI and humans use the same information-filtering rules; AI receives only its own view.

## 7\. Victory, Elimination, and Match Flow

* The last remaining survivor wins.
* If all players die in the same round → draw.
* After a player dies, they no longer act but can continue to spectate in god view:

  * they can see all player positions, terrain, survival status, and public events;
  * no action panel is shown;
  * after the match ends, they still receive the complete replay, which is saved to local match history.
* After resolution, all living humans click "Continue" to proceed to the next round; the result phase auto-continues after 10 seconds.
* When only AI remains, the server simulates directly to the end without waiting for eliminated humans; after the complete record is saved, it automatically resets after 5 seconds and starts the next match.
* There is no disconnect reconnection/automated control during a match; if a non-host disconnects, it is treated as **Hide** and the player dies immediately, with a public broadcast that they "died due to disconnect," and the corpse is recorded normally; if the host disconnects, the room closes.

### Timing

* The host can set the per-round action time in the preparation screen: 20 seconds–3 minutes, default 60 seconds.
* It is locked after the match starts.
* A human who does not submit before the timeout is treated as **Hide**; Hide on a Plain is an empty action, does not make you invisible, and does not block shots.
* AI and humans begin reasoning at the same time; after all humans submit, if the AI has not finished, resolution waits for the AI to finish.

## 8\. Player Count and Match Structure

* **2 players**: one-on-one; little information, direct actions, and bombardment compresses the map relatively quickly.
* **4 players**: threats from multiple directions begin to appear; half-plane clues need to be cross-referenced; recent actions are still relatively easy to track.
* **6–8 players**: more segments and more information; each attack sends a half-plane clue to more players; the probability of early elimination rises; the situation is closer to a multi-party melee.

Player count simultaneously changes:

* the number of map segments and Pillars;
* the number of clues produced by each attack;
* the difficulty of players locating one another;
* the proportion by which bombardment compresses the map;
* the share of the match spent spectating after an early elimination.

## 9\. Basic Strategy

The following are not "optimal solutions," but common decision criteria organized around four dimensions: information, position, action, and risk.

### 9.1 Information Strategy

1. **Observation length trades information against exposure**: The longer the arc, the more segments it covers and the easier it is to find someone; but the observation arc is also the exposure arc—if an attacker is inside it, the observer is easier to hit. The shorter the arc, the less information, but the less profile is exposed.
2. **Prioritize covering the "approach" rather than the "current segment"**: The Slip kill test checks whether the mover's **pre-move segment** is inside the observation arc. Therefore, observation direction and length should prioritize the adjacent segments/approach from which someone might start, rather than merely illuminating your surroundings.
3. **Use historical clues, not just the current round**: Red dots carry round markers; gunshots have left/right counts; deaths and bombardment change position constraints. Multiple clues across rounds can rule out segments and narrow where opponents might be.
4. **Pay attention to the information you emit**: An attack exposes a half-plane; observation does not notify the observed player, but the observation arc itself may be exploited by an attacker; repeatedly acting from the same segment or in the same direction increases the chance of being predicted.
5. **Distinguish "someone is there" from "who is there"**: A red dot means only that someone is present, not identity; a half-plane clue gives only a side, not a distance; you must combine action results, death information, AI behavior, and map structure to infer.

### 9.2 Position Strategy

1. **Tradeoffs between Pillars and Plains**:

   * Pillar: can Hide, and is immune to Observe/Attack while hiding; but can be killed by **Slip** and by bombardment, and observation/movement is less flexible than on a Plain.
   * Plain: cannot Hide; Observe/Attack/Slip all appear in red dots, and there is no cover; but movement can be 2 segments, observation can cover the whole ring, and attack exposure is the whole ring.
   * Pillars offer the option to "counter ranged attacks"; Plains offer "mobility and information."
2. **Avoid staying on a predictable segment for long**: Hiding repeatedly, attacking repeatedly from the same segment, or repeatedly moving in the same direction all make it easier for opponents to cover you with observation or attack arcs.
3. **Keep an exit**: Ruins can cut the ring map; when standing near a one-way door or a dead end, reserve a movement direction for the next round.
4. **Watch the bombardment targets**: Warnings are public; players on target segments die. After the one-quarter threshold, a warning names two targets that explode simultaneously, so you cannot dodge only one. If you are on a target segment, prioritize movement; if you are adjacent to one, consider connectivity after the Ruins form.

### 9.3 Action Choice

1. **Hide**:

   * Good for: predicting that an opponent will use Observe/Attack and your segment is a Pillar;
   * Risk: killed by a single Slip, killed by bombardment; when multiple players Slip in, you may instead survive (current rules);
   * Hide on a Plain provides no cover and is usually just standing by.
2. **Observe**:

   * Good for: confirming whether a direction contains someone, and gathering information for next round's attack/movement;
   * Risk: the longer the arc, the greater the exposure; observation itself causes no damage;
   * Observation results are private and cannot be shared directly.
3. **Attack**:

   * Good for: when you are fairly confident the target will not observe you, or the target is in a state such as Hide/Attack in which it cannot be immune;
   * Risk: exposes a half-plane to all other players; can be interrupted by Slip; in a mutual attack, arc length and terrain affect the outcome;
   * Attack targets a "segment," so a player who Slips in may take the bullet intended for the original occupant.
4. **Slip**:

   * Good for: suspecting a Hide/Attack player in a segment, approaching/escaping, crossing Ruins, or dodging an incoming attack;
   * Risk: an Observer who saw the "approach" can counter-kill; when multiple players are in the same segment, resolution is by movement distance, and you may be eliminated;
   * A single-player Slip and a multi-player Slip can have different outcomes; before acting, confirm whether the landing segment is occupied and whether an Observer is present.

### 9.4 Attack Strategy

1. **Prioritize targets that "cannot be immune"**: Hide (on a Pillar) is immune to Attack; a target that is Observing/Attacking and whose exposure arc contains the attacker is immune to Attack. Therefore, before attacking, judge the target's likely action direction last round/this round.
2. **Use direction and arc length**: Attack direction affects arc length; when both players are on Pillars and exchange fire, the longer-arc side takes no damage from the shorter arc. Pillar-versus-Plain and Plain-versus-Plain exchanges use the ordinary test.
3. **Consider exposure after the attack**: An attack gives every other player a half-plane clue; if multiple players remain, the attack may expose you to several at once. Weigh the "kill payoff" against the risk of being located by a third party.
4. **Do not overlook interruption by Slip**: If the attacker is killed by Slip, the attack is canceled and produces no half-plane exposure; conversely, an attack may also hit a Slip player who has taken the original occupant's place.
5. **Firing results are not fed back**: The attacker knows only the target segment and arc length, not whether the shot was a miss, blocked, a hit, or suppressed; they can infer only from the public death list, corpse discoveries, later observations, and actions. Thus "did this shot hit?" is not immediate confirmation but a hypothesis that requires later verification.

### 9.5 Defensive Strategy

1. **Defense against Observe/Attack**:

   * Hiding on a Pillar is immune to Observe/Attack;
   * When Observing/Attacking, ensure your exposure arc covers segments where an attacker might be;
   * There is no cover on a Plain; you cannot rely on "Hide" for protection.
2. **Defense against Slip**:

   * An observation arc covering the approach can counter-kill a player who Slips in;
   * A Hider is killed by a single Slip; under current rules, when multiple players Slip in, the Hider survives and all who Slip in die;
   * When standing on a Plain, the Slip landing test and multiple-occupant rule still apply.
3. **Defense against bombardment**:

   * Warnings are public; players on a target segment are certain to die, and no action can avoid it;
   * Leave the target segment early, or use Ruins/one-way doors to change an opponent's pursuit route.

### 9.6 Endgame Strategy

1. **One-on-one**:

   * relatively little information, so position and action prediction carry more weight;
   * observation can confirm whether the opponent is in a direction, but a long arc increases the risk of being attacked;
   * when attacking, judge whether the opponent will observe you;
   * Slip can punish Hide/Attack, but an Observer who saw the approach can counter-kill;
   * the bombardment countdown forces both sides to move; they cannot hide indefinitely.
2. **Three or more players**:

   * there are no alliances or communication, but there is a de facto "third-party benefit": attacking one side may let a third party profit;
   * assess who is the current greatest threat, who is most likely to act first, and whether you will be focus-fired;
   * because there is no chat, no binding alliance can be formed; intentions can be expressed or misled only through actions.

### 9.7 AI Matches

* AI and humans use the same action rules and view; AI can see only its own position, observations, and clues.
* The AI model affects its behavioral tendencies; different models have different relative performance in 2/4/8-player games.
* AI does not participate in chat or negotiation; in silent mode, AI and humans have equivalent information conditions.
* Humans can infer an AI's strategy from its action patterns, attack frequency, and Hide/Slip tendencies.

## 10\. Objective Attributes Useful for Evaluation

The following lists facts only, without judging whether they are good or bad:

* Attribute: information structure; In this game: incomplete information; private positions/observations/clues; public map/bombardment/death/half-plane counts
* Attribute: action timing; In this game: all living players secretly choose simultaneously, with unified resolution
* Attribute: actions per round; In this game: 4 types (Hide/Observe/Attack/Slip; Hide on a Plain is an empty action)
* Attribute: sources of randomness; In this game: initial map/spawn points, bombardment targets, AI model and AI actions; action resolution itself has no randomness
* Attribute: player interaction; In this game: direct attack/counter-kill/Slip kill; no trading, no alliances, no chat
* Attribute: elimination mechanism; In this game: 1 HP; after death, a player exits action and can spectate in god view
* Attribute: team structure; In this game: free-for-all, no fixed teams
* Attribute: victory condition; In this game: the last player wins; total elimination is a draw
* Attribute: match length; In this game: determined by the number of rounds, timer, bombardment pacing, and elimination speed; 20–180 seconds per round
* Attribute: player count; In this game: 2–8, humans and AI can be mixed
* Attribute: communication; In this game: none
* Attribute: location of randomness; In this game: mainly in initialization and bombardment; action results are resolved deterministically by the rules
* Attribute: information asymmetry; In this game: each player receives different half-plane constraints; observation results go only to the observer
* Attribute: spectating/review; In this game: eliminated players use god view; a complete replay is saved locally after the match
* Attribute: corpse information; In this game: living players see only corpses in their current segment, know their own discovery round, and do not know the true death time/locations elsewhere; spectating/replay uses god view
* Attribute: AI; In this game: same rules and view filtering as humans; pluggable models; no social communication

### 10.1 Formal Properties from a Game-Theory Perspective

* **Finite players, free-for-all**: 2–8 players, no fixed teams, no binding alliances;
* **Non-cooperative, no communication**: players cannot negotiate, trade, vote, or share private information;
* **Simultaneous actions**: each round, all living players submit actions without knowing others' current choices;
* **Incomplete information**: players know only their own position, observations, and clues; opponents' positions and actions are hidden states;
* **Private signals**: observation red dots, half-plane clues, and one's own damage/death are sent only to the relevant player; firing results are not fed back;
* **Deterministic state transitions**: action resolution is determined by a fixed order and geometric relations, with no action dice;
* **Sources of randomness**: initial map/spawn points, bombardment targets, AI model selection, and AI actions;
* **Elimination as an absorbing state**: a dead player exits action; the last player wins; total elimination is a draw;
* **Single-match winner-takes-all**: there are no in-match points or second-place payoff; victory is winner-takes-all;
* **Mixed-strategy space**: because of simultaneous actions and hidden information, players can randomize actions to avoid being predicted;
* **No side-effect commitments**: there is no chat, so there is no "promise–betrayal" mechanism; actions themselves are the only signal.

## 11\. One-Round Decision Checklist

Each round, you can ask yourself in the following order:

1. What is the terrain at my current position? Pillar/Plain/Ruins adjacency?
2. What did I do last round? What might opponents infer from it about me?
3. Which red dots, half-plane clues, and left/right gunshot counts did I receive?
4. Which segments are most likely to contain opponents? Which segments are most likely to contain someone observing/attacking me?
5. Where is this round's bombardment target? Do I need to move?
6. If I choose Hide/Observe/Attack/Slip, what do I expose, gain, and avoid in each case?
7. If opponents simultaneously choose Observe/Attack/Slip, how will my action resolve?
8. Do I have a safe landing segment/retreat direction?
9. How many players remain? Am I likely to become a focus-fire target for a third party?
10. Is this round for gathering information, preserving myself, or trying for a kill?

## 12\. Appendix: Easily Misread Resolutions in the Current Rules

To avoid misreadings during evaluation, these are listed separately:

* **1 player who Slips in** enters the segment of a Hider/Attacker: the Hider/Attacker dies.
* **2 or more players who Slip in** enter the same segment: resolution is by movement distance; a stationary player has distance 0 and usually survives; all who Slip in die.
* **Hide on a Plain** is not invisible and does not block Observe/Attack, but when 1 player who Slips in enters, the stationary player is still killed.
* **"Observe counters Slip"** is judged by whether the observation arc contains the Slip player's **pre-move** segment, not the landing segment or transit segments.
* **"Attack counters Observe"** is judged by whether the observer's exposure arc contains the attacker's segment; if it contains, no damage; if it does not contain, damage.
* **Attack arc length** is compared only when both players are on Pillars and exchange fire; a full-ring arc from a Plain does not enjoy mutual-attack protection.
* **Bombardment** ignores Hide/Observe/Attack/Slip; players on the target segment die directly.
* **"Shortest distance stays"** in the multiple-occupant rule means that "increasing the number of players who Slip in" changes the direction of the outcome; this is an explicit resolution under the current rules, not randomness.
